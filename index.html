Project 01

LeafDoc

Smartphone plant imaging system with AI-powered diagnosis

  • SolidWorks
  • Kotlin
  • CameraX
  • Image Stitching
  • 3D Printing

A $50 handheld lightbox that clips onto a phone, blocks ambient light, and lights the leaf evenly. It turns an ordinary smartphone into a repeatable imaging rig.

Problem

  • Ambient light makes phone-captured leaf images inconsistent and hard to compare.
  • Holding a leaf by hand introduces curl, shadow, and framing variation on every shot.

What I built

  • Contoured rollers flatten and center the leaf as it enters, so the positioning repeats without any manual adjustment.
  • MagSafe mounting locks the phone in a fixed position above the sample.
  • Seven enclosure revisions in SolidWorks, each one printed to test light leak, roller clearance, and component fit.
  • A native Android app in Kotlin and CameraX, with manual camera control and an alignment stitching algorithm.

Result

  • Overlapping frames assemble into a single full-length leaf image automatically.
  • Cloud AI returns a health score, primary finding, and confidence level inside the app, with no export step or outside tools.
Project 02

FluoroScan

Portable photosynthetic efficiency imaging system

  • Raspberry Pi
  • SolidWorks
  • Optics
  • LED Arrays
  • Fluorescence

Direct, non-invasive measurement of photosynthetic efficiency using chlorophyll fluorescence. It catches plant stress early and costs far less than a commercial instrument.

The physics

  • A weak, non-actinic light opens the PSII reaction centers and gives the baseline fluorescence, Fo.
  • A saturating pulse closes all of them and gives the maximum fluorescence, Fm.
  • The ratio Fv/Fm, where Fv = Fm − Fo, is the maximum efficiency of PSII energy conversion.

What I built

  • Prototyped in SolidWorks and printed on Bambu Lab hardware.
  • A Raspberry Pi drives the camera and system control.
  • 450 nm excitation at 4000 µmol m⁻²s⁻¹ from 12 LEDs in a 3-up configuration on an angled mount.
  • Emission captured through a 680/720 nm filter after dark adaptation.

Result

  • Real-time imaging catches stress before it shows up to the eye.
  • Cheap enough that a lab without a commercial fluorometer can still take the measurement.
Project 03

Multispectral Handheld Imaging Device

Field-portable plant phenotyping instrument

  • Raspberry Pi
  • Electronics
  • MOSFET Switching
  • Reverse Engineering
  • SolidWorks

A phenotyping device that captures leaf detail at 0.04 mm/pixel across six wavelength bands. I redesigned it so that every component fits inside the enclosure.

Problem

  • The original kept the Raspberry Pi in an external housing, with wiring running outside the body.
  • None of it was documented, so every subsystem had to be traced before anything could change.

What I built

  • Reverse-engineered and documented every subsystem, then rebuilt the enclosure so all the electronics sit inside it.
  • 120+ LEDs hand-soldered across 6 wavelength bands.
  • MOSFET transistor switching with 12V/5V power conversion.
  • Raspberry Pi for camera control and capture sequencing.

Result

  • A high signal-to-noise ratio with ambient light fully blocked, resolving the secondary leaf veins.
  • Detected plant stress days before visual symptoms appeared.
  • Gave a co-researcher's ML model the data to reach 90% classification accuracy in a peer-reviewed publication, which I am a co-author on.
Project 04 In progress

Leaf Cross-Section Microscope Imager

M.S. project, Purdue Plant Sensor Lab · Jan 2026 to present

  • Optics
  • Liquid Lens
  • Motor Control
  • Web Console
  • SolidWorks

A modular attachment for the lab's handheld 400× leaf microscope that images a full corn-leaf cross section, so nitrogen status can be read from the leaf's internal structure.

Problem

  • The microscope images a single spot at a time, so a full cross section cannot be captured in one go.
  • Its motor-and-gear focusing stage was too slow and too unstable for work at this magnification.

What I built

  • A motor-driven clamp and stage that moves the leaf laterally through the optical path, capturing the cross section frame by frame at 200×.
  • An electrically tunable liquid lens in place of the old focusing stage, removing the moving parts from focus entirely.
  • A web-based control console for lighting, motor motion, live view, capture and labelling, replacing a terminal-only workflow.
  • A desktop app that stitches the frames into a single image, with manual alignment, colour correction and side-by-side comparison.

Where it stands

  • The stage, liquid lens focusing and control console are built and running.
  • Still in active development, so there are no published results yet.
