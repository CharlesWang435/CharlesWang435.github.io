Project 01

LeafDoc

Smartphone plant imaging system with AI-powered diagnosis

  • SolidWorks
  • Kotlin
  • CameraX
  • Image Stitching
  • 3D Printing

A $50 handheld lightbox that clips onto a phone, blocks ambient light, and delivers uniform LED illumination — turning any smartphone into a repeatable leaf imaging rig.

The Problem

  • Ambient light makes phone-captured leaf images inconsistent and hard to compare.
  • Hand-holding a leaf introduces curl, shadow, and framing variation on every shot.

What I Built

  • Contoured rollers flatten and center the leaf on entry — repeatable positioning, no manual adjustment.
  • MagSafe mounting locks the phone in a fixed position above the sample.
  • 7 enclosure revisions in SolidWorks, each printed to test light leak, roller clearance, and component fit.
  • Native Android app in Kotlin and CameraX with manual camera control and an alignment stitching algorithm.

Outcome

  • Overlapping frames assemble into a single full-length leaf image automatically.
  • Cloud AI returns a health score, primary finding, and confidence level in-app — no export, no external tools.
Project 02

FluroScan

Portable photosynthetic efficiency imaging system

  • Raspberry Pi
  • SolidWorks
  • Optics
  • LED Arrays
  • Fluorescence

Direct, non-invasive measurement of photosynthetic efficiency via chlorophyll fluorescence — detecting plant stress early, at a fraction of the cost of commercial instrumentation.

The Physics

  • Weak non-actinic light opens PSII reaction centers, giving baseline fluorescence Fo.
  • A saturating pulse closes all centers, giving maximum fluorescence Fm.
  • The ratio Fv/Fm, where Fv = Fm − Fo, is the maximum efficiency of PSII energy conversion.

The Build

  • Prototyped in SolidWorks, printed on Bambu Lab hardware.
  • Raspberry Pi drives the camera and system control.
  • 450 nm excitation at 4000 µmol m⁻²s⁻¹ from 12 LEDs in a 3-up configuration on an angled mount.
  • Emission captured through a 680/720 nm filter after dark adaptation.

Why It Matters

  • Real-time imaging catches stress before it is visible to the eye.
  • Affordable and accessible enough to put this measurement in far more hands.
Project 03

Multispectral Handheld Imaging Device

Field-portable plant phenotyping instrument

  • Raspberry Pi
  • Electronics
  • MOSFET Switching
  • Reverse Engineering
  • SolidWorks

A phenotyping device capturing leaf detail at 0.04 mm/pixel across six wavelength bands — redesigned so every electronic component lives inside the enclosure.

The Redesign

  • The original housed the Raspberry Pi externally, with wiring trailing out of the body.
  • I reverse-engineered and documented every subsystem, then rebuilt the enclosure to house all electronics internally — a genuinely field-portable instrument.

The Electronics

  • 120+ LEDs hand-soldered across 6 wavelength bands.
  • MOSFET transistor switching with 12V/5V power conversion.
  • Raspberry Pi for camera control and capture sequencing.

Results

  • High signal-to-noise ratio with ambient light fully blocked; secondary leaf veins clearly resolved.
  • Detected plant stress days before visual symptoms appeared.
  • Enabled a co-researcher's ML model to reach 90% classification accuracy in a peer-reviewed publication — co-author.
