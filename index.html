Project 01 In progress

Leaf Cross-Section Microscope Imager

M.S. project, Purdue ABE Plant Sensor Lab · Jan 2026 to present

A modular attachment for the lab's handheld 400× leaf microscope that images a full corn-leaf cross section, enabling nitrogen estimation from internal leaf structure.

  • Optics
  • Liquid Lens
  • 3D Printing
  • Motor Control
  • Web Console
  • SolidWorks

One corn leaf, edge → midrib · ~40 frames stitched · captured by prototype 1

Problem

  • The microscope sees one spot at a time, and its autofocus hunts for peak sharpness by stepping the leaf platform down a threaded-rod stage, taking about a minute per frame.
  • Play in the platform's guide rods tilts each shot slightly. That is negligible on a leaf surface, but enough to push a thin cross section out of the frame entirely.
  • Autofocus scores sharpness at the center of the view, and a cut corn leaf is rarely centered, so it often locks onto the empty stage instead of the leaf.

Prototype 1 · built

  • A 3D-printed clamp on a motorized linear rail carries the cut leaf sideways under the objective in 0.75 mm steps, cross section facing the lens.
  • A web control console (Leafspec‑DCM) adds live camera preview and real-time motor control, replacing a terminal-only workflow.
  • 21 corn-leaf cross sections captured edge to midrib, each stitched from around 40 frames in a companion desktop app.
  • The catch: focusing each frame by hand took roughly 20 seconds, so one leaf took over 13 minutes.

Prototype 2 · in progress

  • An electrically tunable liquid lens, already fitted into the optical path, focuses with no moving parts, so the platform tilt no longer matters.
  • The motor freed from the focusing gears now drives a weight-balanced rack-and-pinion stage, replacing the off-center linear actuator whose overhung weight tilted the stage and let in stray light.
  • Target: a full cross-section scan in under 1 minute per leaf, down from 13.

Project 02

LeafDoc

Smartphone plant imaging system with in-app AI diagnosis

A $50 handheld lightbox that blocks ambient light and lights the leaf evenly. A phone snaps onto the top surface, ready to image through the LeafDoc app, turning an ordinary smartphone into a professional imaging rig.

  • SolidWorks
  • Kotlin
  • CameraX
  • Image Stitching
  • 3D Printing

Problem

  • Ambient light makes phone-captured leaf images inconsistent and hard to compare.
  • Holding a leaf by hand introduces curl, shadow, and framing variation on every shot.

What I built

  • Contoured rollers flatten and center the leaf as it enters, so the positioning repeats without any manual adjustment.
  • MagSafe mounting locks the phone in a fixed position above the sample.
  • Seven enclosure revisions in SolidWorks, each one printed to test light leak, roller clearance, and component fit.
  • A native Android app in Kotlin and CameraX, with manual camera control and an alignment stitching algorithm.

Result

  • Overlapping frames assemble into a single full-length leaf image automatically.
  • Cloud AI returns a health score, primary finding, and confidence level inside the app, with no export step or outside tools.
Project 03

FluoroScan

Portable photosynthetic efficiency imaging system

Under $2,000 in parts, built to measure photosynthetic efficiency directly and non-invasively through chlorophyll fluorescence, so plant stress could be caught before it is visible.

  • Raspberry Pi
  • SolidWorks
  • Optics
  • LED Arrays
  • Fluorescence

The physics

  • After dark adaptation, every PSII reaction center sits open; a weak, non-actinic light reads the baseline fluorescence, Fo, without closing them.
  • A saturating pulse closes all of them and gives the maximum fluorescence, Fm.
  • The ratio Fv/Fm, where Fv = Fm − Fo, is the maximum efficiency of PSII energy conversion.

What I built

  • Twelve 450 nm LEDs on hand-soldered star boards, angled 3-up at the sample plane so the whole leaf lights evenly.
  • 4000 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ at the sample, the calculated target for closing every PSII centre at once — designed, not yet measured.
  • A 680/720 nm bandpass that passes the fluorescence and rejects the far brighter excitation.
  • One timed sequence fires the flash and trips the shutter, so the frame lands while the centres are still closed.

The goal

  • Real-time imaging that flags stress before it shows up to the eye.
  • A build cost low enough that a lab without a commercial fluorometer can still take the measurement. The camera, lens and emission filter are most of the bill.

Project 04

Multispectral Handheld Imaging Device

Field-portable multispectral plant phenotyping instrument

A phenotyping device that captures leaf detail at 0.04 mm/pixel across six wavelength bands. I redesigned it so that every component fits inside the enclosure.

  • Raspberry Pi
  • 3D Printing
  • MOSFET Switching
  • Reverse Engineering
  • SolidWorks

Problem

  • The original kept the Raspberry Pi in an external housing, with wiring running outside the body.
  • None of it was documented, so every subsystem had to be traced before anything could change.

What I built

  • Reverse-engineered and documented every subsystem, then rebuilt the enclosure so all the electronics sit inside it.
  • 120+ LEDs hand-soldered across 6 wavelength bands.
  • MOSFET switching with 12V/5V power conversion.
  • Raspberry Pi for camera control and capture sequencing.

Result

  • A high signal-to-noise ratio with ambient light fully blocked, resolving the secondary leaf veins.
  • Gave a co-researcher's ML model the data to reach 90% classification accuracy in a peer-reviewed publication I co-authored.

Switch between the two builds
